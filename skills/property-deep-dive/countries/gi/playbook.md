# Gibraltar 🇬🇮 — Property Due-Diligence Playbook

ISO2: `gi`. Status: ✅ Standalone playbook (UK Overseas Territory; own tax authority, land registry, courts).

## Country profile

- Postcode: single Gibraltar code `GX11 1AA` (covers the entire territory; not granular like UK postcodes)
- Area: ~6.8 km²; population ~32,700 (Gibraltar Statistics Office, 2023)
- Currency: Gibraltar Pound (GIP), 1:1 pegged to GBP; sterling and GIP both legal tender locally; only GBP accepted in UK/EU
- Language: English (official); Spanish + Llanito widely spoken
- Legal system: English common law (with Gibraltar-specific Acts); Court of Appeal + Supreme Court of Gibraltar; ultimate appeal to JCPC (Judicial Committee of the Privy Council, London)
- Land registry: **Land Titles Registry** under the [Gibraltar Land Titles Act 2011](https://www.gibraltarlaws.gov.gi/legislations/gibraltar-land-titles-act-2011-3009) (Registrar of Land Titles appointed under Notice 2011/206; register governed by the Gibraltar Land Titles (Register) Regulations 2023 and the Land Registry Portal and Electronic Stamping Act 2023), operated by **Land Property Services Ltd** ([lps.gi](https://www.lps.gi/)) (2026 data, source gibraltarlaws.gov.gi + lps.gi; 2026-09-18 verified)
- EU status: **OUTSIDE EU** since 31 January 2020 (left with UK). Schengen border rules apply at Gibraltar's external border under the UK–EU Gibraltar Treaty — **provisionally applied since 15 July 2026** ([EU Council greenlight 01 Apr 2026](https://www.consilium.europa.eu/en/press/press-releases/2026/04/01/eu-uk-relations-member-states-greenlight-eu-uk-deal-on-gibraltar/); see § Frontier Treaty below)

⚠️ **Do not conflate**: Gibraltar is a **UK Overseas Territory**, not part of the UK itself and not part of Spain. Gibraltar has its own tax authority (Gibraltar Income Tax Office), its own GFSC-regulated banks, its own Supreme Court — references to "HMRC" or "Land Registry England & Wales" do **not** apply.

## Section: `--foreign-buyer-rules`

**Data sources** (priority order):

1. **Gibraltar Income Tax Office — Qualifying Individuals**: [gibraltar.gov.gi/income-tax-office/individuals-and-employees/qualifying-individuals](https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/income-tax-office/individuals-and-employees/qualifying-individuals)
2. **Gibraltar Finance Centre (Cat-2/HEPSS administration)**: [gibraltarfinance.gi](https://www.gibraltarfinance.gi/) — Suite 761 Europort, +350 20050011
3. **Gibraltar laws portal (primary statutes)**: [gibraltarlaws.gov.gi](https://www.gibraltarlaws.gov.gi/)
4. **Estate-agent market-class signposting** (Open vs Restricted; agent pages, listing-platform tier): [Chestertons — 3 year residency](https://www.chestertons.gi/3yr-residency) ("also known as the local market or restricted market"; owner and immediate family only, no letting), [BFA — Restricted Market](https://bfagib.com/restricted-market-details) (purchase needs Land Property Services approval; developments grouped A–C), [Richardsons buying guide](https://www.richardsonsproperties.com/blog/richardsons-guide-buying-property-gibraltar-413) ("Anyone (citizen or non-citizen) can buy on the open market"). Century 21 Gibraltar's site blocks scripted access, so its signposting was not re-checked; no "1704 Luxury Real Estate" website or directory listing was found (2026-10-29 verified)

**Key rules — TWO-TIER residential property market**:

Gibraltar operates a **two-tier residential property market**. Failing to identify which tier a listing sits in is the single most consequential error a foreign buyer can make. A substantial share of stock (est. ~half per estate-agent signposting — no published register split; verify) is **not** accessible to non-residents.

- **Open Market** — no nationality or residency restriction; any buyer eligible. Carries a price premium versus Restricted-Market stock at comparable size/location.
- **Restricted Market** (a.k.a. "3-year residency" market / pre-owned Government Affordable Housing / certain estate-by-estate covenants) — purchaser must demonstrate **≥3 years continuous, ordinary Gibraltar residency immediately prior to purchase**; **owner-occupation only**; resale also restricted (subject to the same eligibility test for successor buyers, and to the Affordable Housing buyback regime where applicable). Non-residents, Cat-2/HEPSS pre-arrival applicants, and overseas investors are **categorically excluded** from this tier.

**Foreign-buyer reality**: non-residents and Cat-2/HEPSS pre-arrival applicants are **effectively limited to the Open Market**. Restricted-Market listings often appear at headline-attractive prices but **cannot be acquired without 3-year prior Gibraltar residency**.

**Verification path BEFORE making an offer**:
- Confirm each listing's market class with the **estate agent in writing** AND cross-check with the **title deed** (any covenant referencing residency eligibility / Government buyback / Affordable Housing scheme tier = Restricted)
- Confirm current residency category eligibility with **Gibraltar Finance Centre** (info@financecentre.gov.gi, +350 20050011) — Cat-2 / HEPSS / Self-Sufficiency / Employment
- Confirm cross-border tax position with both Gibraltar Income Tax Office AND home-country revenue (Gibraltar has **no tax treaty network of the breadth UK has**; check bilateral arrangements)

**Other foreign-buyer notes**:

- **Residency is decoupled from ownership** (even on the Open Market) — buying property does NOT confer right to live in Gibraltar. Buyers without a Gibraltar identity card (any nationality, British included — the Residency Regulations 2026 contain no nationality criterion) need a separate **residence permit** under the Residency Regulations 2026 (LN 166/2026, from 14 Jul 2026): employee / self-employed / student / existing or permanent resident / Government discretion. Cat-2 and HEPSS are tax certificates; there is no self-sufficiency route until the over-55 amendment announced in HMGoG PR 813/2026 (21 Oct 2026) is made. How a new Cat-2 or HEPSS applicant gets a residence permit is not stated in LN 166/2026 — verify with the Department of Immigration and Home Affairs.
- **Self-employment route is discretionary and frequently refused** — community signal: r/gibraltar 1opdfxf (Q2 2026), where an applicant detailed refusal despite 12-month residential + office leases in place. Engage a Gibraltar-licensed immigration lawyer **pre-relocation**; do **not** rely on a property purchase or lease as evidence of eligibility.
- **EU/EEA buyers post-Brexit**: Free-movement rights from EU into Gibraltar **ended 31 Jan 2020** (Brexit). From Brexit until the Treaty's provisional application, EU/EEA nationals required entry under the same routes as third-country nationals; **since 15 Jul 2026 entry follows Schengen border rules at Gibraltar's airport/port under the UK–EU Treaty — residence rights are NOT granted** (Gibraltar's own immigration routes still required; [FCDO treaty summary](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/draft-uk-eu-agreement-in-respect-of-gibraltar/summary-draft-uk-eu-agreement-in-respect-of-gibraltar-accessible)).
- **Frontier Treaty implications**: Since provisional application (15 Jul 2026), routine immigration checks and physical barriers at the land border (La Línea/La Verja crossing) are removed and Schengen checks sit at Gibraltar's airport and port (dual controls — Gibraltar immigration + Spanish officials for Schengen-area integrity, per the FCDO summary); daily cross-frontier workers from Spain (14,112 frontier-worker employee jobs in October 2025, before the treaty — employees normally resident in Spain, counted as employee jobs reported by employers; October 2025 data, source [Employment Survey Report 2025](https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/uploads/statistics/2025/Reports/Employment%20Survey%20Report%202025.pdf) Table 2.4; 2026-10-29 verified) gain streamlined crossing. **Treaty does NOT auto-grant Schengen residency rights to Gibraltar property owners** — see § Frontier Treaty for verbatim Government statement.

**Confidence**: HIGH on the two-tier (Open / Restricted) market framework (4 estate-agent sources + r/AskUK community confirmation). HIGH on Brexit/Schengen status. HIGH on self-employment refusal pattern (community signal + corroborated by absent-Cat-2 enforcement chatter). MEDIUM-HIGH on Frontier Treaty operational details (provisional application live since 15 Jul 2026 per HMGoG implementation releases; full ratification — European Parliament consent (indicative plenary 14 Dec 2026, EPRS) + UK CRAG scrutiny (first objection period ends 22 Oct 2026, Commons Library CBP-10980) — still outstanding; re-verify at gibraltar.gov.gi closer to purchase).

## Section: `--tax`

**Statutory framework**: **Income Tax Act 2010** (consolidated at [gibraltarlaws.gov.gi](https://www.gibraltarlaws.gov.gi/)) administered by **Gibraltar Income Tax Office**. Subsidiary legislation cited below — **Qualifying (Category 2) Individuals Rules 2004** and **High Executive Possessing Specialist Skills Rules 2008** — both made under the Income Tax Act 2010.

### Income tax — dual system

Gibraltar operates **two parallel income tax systems**; the taxpayer elects whichever produces lower tax (Income Tax Office, 2025/26 rates per [PwC Worldwide Tax Summaries — Gibraltar Individual](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/gibraltar/individual/taxes-on-personal-income), last updated 10 March 2026):

**Allowance-Based System (ABS)** — taxable income after personal allowances:
- First **£4,000**: 14%
- Next **£12,000**: 17%
- Balance: **39%**
- Tax-exempt threshold: £11,450; taper relief £11,451–£19,500

**Gross Income Based (GIB) system** — applies to gross income (no major allowances), tiered bands:

For gross income ≤ £25,000:
- First £10,000: 6%
- Next £7,000: 20%
- Balance: 28%

For gross income £25,001–£100,000 (assessable):
- First £17,000: 16%
- Next £8,000: 19%
- Next £15,000: 25%
- Next £65,000: 28%
- Remainder: 25%

(Higher-band structure for assessable income >£100,000 also exists — verify at gibraltar.gov.gi/income-tax-office.)

### What Gibraltar does NOT have

Confirmed by [PwC Gibraltar — Other Taxes](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/gibraltar/individual/other-taxes) (2025/26):
- **No VAT** ("There is no VAT in Gibraltar")
- **No Capital Gains Tax** (on personal-asset disposals including property — verify scope at gibraltar.gov.gi)
- **No Wealth Tax**
- **No Inheritance Tax / Estate Duty**
- **No Gift Tax**

This is Gibraltar's distinguishing tax draw versus UK / Spain / most of EU. ⚠️ **Caveat**: tax-residency-of-origin matters. A UK-domiciled buyer remains subject to UK IHT on worldwide assets including Gibraltar property unless domicile changes — separate from Gibraltar's domestic rules.

### Stamp Duty on property (Stamp Duties Act, amended by Act No. 37 of 2024, effective 23 December 2024)

**Standard residential rates** ([PwC Gibraltar Other Taxes](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/gibraltar/individual/other-taxes)):
- ≤ £200,000: **0%**
- £200,001–£350,000: **2%** on first £250,000 + **5.5%** on balance
- £350,001–£800,000: **3%** on first £350,000 + **3.5%** on balance
- ≥ £800,001: **3%** on first £350,000 + **3.5%** on next £450,000 + **4.5%** on balance

**First/second-time buyer relief**: 0% on the first **£300,000** of the price of a single residential dwelling, then 5.5% (£300k–£350k) / 3.5% (£350k–£800k) / 4.5% (above £800k) (Stamp Duties Act 2005 consolidated 23 Dec 2024, first/second-time-buyer para (1A)) (threshold raised from £260k → £300k effective 11 July 2023). PENDING, NOT ENACTED: Stamp Duties and Gibraltar Land Titles (Amendment) Bill 2025 (B05/2025, Gazette No. 5218, 27 Mar 2025) is still on the Parliament agenda of 14 Oct 2026 (Committee Stage and Third Reading); as published it would limit this relief to property not exceeding £800,000 (deleting the 4.5% tail), add a 0.5% vendor-paid duty on off-plan purchase agreements and later deeds of assignment and novation (new s.19D), and treat an individual over 65 downsizing a principal home as a first-time buyer (new s.19AA) — the bill may change before enactment ([Bill B05/2025](https://gibraltarlaws.gov.gi/uploads/bills/bills2025/2025B05.pdf); 2026-10-21 verified)

**Affordable Housing scheme**: 0% on initial purchase; **7.5% on resale within 10 years** (Stamp Duties Act s.19B: applies to Beach View Terraces / Mons Calpe Mews resales within 10 years of the first underlease assignment; payable by the vendor on top of ordinary duty; the Land Management Committee may waive it for a forced sale or a meritorious upgrade — divorce and death are not listed grounds) (2024 data, source Stamp Duties Act 2005 consolidated 23 Dec 2024)

**Mortgage stamp duty**:
- Up to and including £200,000: 0.13% of secured sum
- Above £200,000: 0.20%

**Spouse-to-spouse transfers**: nil duty

**Worked example** (£500,000 standard purchase, not first-time buyer):
- First £350,000 @ 3% = £10,500
- Balance £150,000 @ 3.5% = £5,250
- **Total stamp duty: £15,750** (= 3.15% effective)

(Verify: confirm tier interaction edge cases — the "between bands" structure means rate cliff-edges exist near £350k and £800k; consult a Gibraltar conveyancer for tier-boundary purchases.)

### Annual rates (general rates)

Levied by **Government of Gibraltar Treasury / Rates Office** on domestic and commercial property; **payable quarterly**. Calculated on the **net annual value** of the property as assessed by **Land Property Services** (Angloinfo confirms structure; verify exact band-by-band tariff at primary source). Residential premises receive a **10% discount** versus commercial.

Net annual value reassessments are issued by the Land Property Services Office; appeal route runs to the Rates Tribunal. ⚠️ Tariff-per-pound-of-NAV for 2025/26 is **not publicly indexed in one canonical location** — request the seller's most recent rates demand pre-purchase AND confirm current tariff with Gibraltar Treasury / Land Property Services directly.

Sources: [PwC Gibraltar Other Taxes](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/gibraltar/individual/other-taxes), [Angloinfo Gibraltar — General Taxes](https://www.angloinfo.com/how-to/gibraltar/money/gibraltar-taxes), [EY Gibraltar Tax Facts 2025–2026](https://www.ey.com/content/dam/ey-unified-site/ey-com/en-gi/tax-alerts/documents/ey-gibraltar-tax-facts-2025-2026.pdf).

### Category 2 (Cat-2) Individual — high-net-worth resident scheme

Statutory basis: **Qualifying (Category 2) Individuals Rules 2004** (subsidiary legislation under the Income Tax Act 2010 — [gibraltarlaws.gov.gi](https://www.gibraltarlaws.gov.gi/)).

Source: [Gibraltar Income Tax Office — Qualifying Individuals](https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/income-tax-office/individuals-and-employees/qualifying-individuals) + [Hassans International](https://www.gibraltarlaw.com/expertise/private-client/category-2-status-in-gibraltar/) (verbatim wording confirmed) + Gibraltar Finance Centre administration:

- **Minimum net worth**: **"at least an estimated total net worth in excess of £2 million"** (Hassans; the consolidated Qualifying (Category 2) Individuals Rules 2004 set no numeric figure — rule 6(a) requires only 'substantial and sound financial standing') — **£5 million for NEW applicants announced** per [HMGoG PR 469/2026](https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/press-releases/government-announces-changes-to-category-2-regime-4692026-12107) (18 Jun 2026; existing Cat-2 holders grandfathered; the only amending instrument, Qualifying (Category 2) Individuals (Amendment) Rules 2026 (LN 127/2026, Gazette No. 5316, 22 Jun 2026, in operation on publication — [gibraltarlaws.gov.gi](https://www.gibraltarlaws.gov.gi/legislations/qualifying-category-2-individuals-amendment-rules-2026-8469)), changes the application fee only, so the £5M is an administrative threshold, not legislated — confirm with Gibraltar Finance Centre; 2026-10-21 verified)
- **Taxation method**: ABS only (Cat-2s cannot elect GIB)
- **Worldwide taxable income cap**: **£118,000/yr** (taxed only on first £118k regardless of true global income — 2025/26)
- **Minimum annual tax**: **£37,000**
- **Maximum annual tax**: **£42,380** (2025/26)
- **Residency conditions**: must maintain approved residential accommodation in Gibraltar **for the whole year of assessment** (purchased OR rented; cannot be sub-let to third parties); must NOT have been resident in Gibraltar in the **5 years immediately preceding** the year of assessment (≥183 days in any single year, or average ≥90 days across 3 of the 5 years = "resident" for this test)
- **Application pack** (per Hassans verbatim): CV + certified passport copy + evidence of net worth (£2M+ per Hassans; £5M+ for new applicants per HMGoG PR 469/2026) + private medical insurance + 2 character references (one from a banker) + proof of approved accommodation
- **Fees**: **£5,000 non-refundable application fee** (raised from £1,233 by LN 127/2026, rule 8(1)(a), in operation 22 Jun 2026; announced HMGoG PR 469/2026, 18 Jun 2026) + **£42,380 refundable deposit** (matches the maximum annual tax)
- **Certificate issued by**: Gibraltar Finance, Suite 761 Europort, +350 20050011, info@financecentre.gov.gi

### HEPSS (High Executive Possessing Specialist Skills)

Statutory basis: **High Executive Possessing Specialist Skills Rules 2008** (subsidiary legislation under the Income Tax Act 2010 — [gibraltarlaws.gov.gi](https://www.gibraltarlaws.gov.gi/legislations/high-executive-possessing-specialist-skills-rules-2008-2152/download)).

- **Minimum Gibraltar earnings**: **> £160,000/yr** (2025/26)
- **Assessable income cap**: **first £160,000** only — income above is not assessed
- **Fixed annual tax liability**: **£39,940** (2025/26) regardless of actual earnings above £160k
- **Skills test**: skills must "promote and sustain economic activity of particular value to Gibraltar" and **not be readily available** within Gibraltar — Government discretion via Gibraltar Finance Centre
- **Residency conditions**: exclusive use of approved residential accommodation in Gibraltar for the whole year of assessment; must NOT have been resident or working in Gibraltar within the **3 years preceding** application (note: 3 years for HEPSS vs 5 years for Cat-2)
- **Both Cat-2 and HEPSS certificates issued by Gibraltar Finance** (not automatic; case-by-case approval)

⚠️ **Verification path**: Cat-2 and HEPSS thresholds change with the annual budget — re-verify minimum tax, max tax, income cap, fees, and skills test at [gibraltar.gov.gi/income-tax-office](https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/income-tax-office) and the consolidated **Qualifying (Category 2) Individuals Rules 2004** / **HEPSS Rules 2008** at [gibraltarlaws.gov.gi](https://www.gibraltarlaws.gov.gi/) before relying on any figure. Community signal (r/gibraltar 1tkeoa5, Q2 2026) flagged Cat-2 / residency rule changes — now primary-confirmed: Cat-2 new-applicant net wealth £2M → £5M + fee £1,233 → £5,000 (HMGoG PR 469/2026, 18 Jun 2026), and the Residency Regulations 2026 (LN 166/2026) in operation since 14 Jul 2026.

**Confidence**: HIGH for stamp duty bands and the "no VAT / no IHT / no wealth tax / no CGT" framework (PwC + cross-checked vs Stamp Duties Amendment Act 37/2024). HIGH for the Cat-2 £5,000 fee (LN 127/2026); MEDIUM for the £5M new-applicant net worth (announced in HMGoG PR 469/2026; administrative, not in the Rules text; existing holders grandfathered at £2M); HIGH for Cat-2 ( £118k cap, £37k–£42,380 tax band, £42,380 deposit — Hassans + Income Tax Office). HIGH for HEPSS (£160k earnings floor, £160k assessable cap, £39,940 fixed annual tax — cross-confirmed multiple Gibraltar professional sources + statutory Rules 2008).

## Section: `--ownership-and-tenure`

**Data sources**: [Gibraltar laws portal](https://www.gibraltarlaws.gov.gi/) (primary statute lookup); **Land Titles Registry (LPS)**; Land Property Services.

**Tenure types** in Gibraltar:

1. **Freehold** — outright ownership; least common in central Gibraltar due to limited land
2. **Crown leasehold** — substantial portion of Gibraltar's developed land is held on **long lease from the Crown** (Government of Gibraltar). Typical lease lengths: 99, 125, or 150 years. Ground rent often nominal.
3. **Private leasehold** — sub-lease from a freeholder (less common)
4. **Commonhold** — rare; verify specific scheme applicability

**Pre-contract checks** (engage Gibraltar-qualified conveyancer/barrister — see § notary section):

- Confirm tenure type with a copy of the title deed
- Confirm **market-class designation** (Open vs Restricted — see § `--foreign-buyer-rules`)
- Crown lease: verify unexpired term, ground rent, service charge, renewal/extension rights, alterations consent regime
- Service charges for apartments (most central Gibraltar stock): confirm budget + reserve fund status
- Any Crown lease covenant restricting sublet / short-let / commercial use

**Affordable housing scheme**: Government-subsidised housing schemes (Mons Calpe Mews, etc.) sit in the **Restricted Market** tier and carry **resale restrictions for 10 years**; the 7.5% special stamp duty on resale within 10 years (Stamp Duties Act s.19B, paid by the vendor) applies only to Beach View Terraces and Mons Calpe Mews; the schemes also carry a buyback right to Government. Confirm the scheme tier before purchase.

**Confidence**: MEDIUM — tenure-type framework is well-established, but lease-length and ground-rent specifics are property-by-property; require title deed review.

- Planning and spatial data: all development needs planning permission and "The decision-making authority is the Development & Planning Commission" ([Town Planning](https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/town-planning)); search past applications for the plot and its neighbours on [e-Planning](https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/town-planning/e-planning) (online since 13 Oct 2015); maps on the HMGoG [GeoPortal](https://www.geoportal.gov.gi/) (Town Planning Viewer, INSPIRE viewer) (2026-10-29 verified)

**Flood**: Gibraltar has no rivers, and a 2011 HMGoG flood risk assessment found groundwater flooding "unlikely to occur in Gibraltar"; flash flooding from intense rain "is not uncommon" — the CCVRA 2022 rates its current probability medium-high and magnitude medium-low, both expected to increase. In heavy rain Gibraltar sees "major road arteries becoming flooded and storm drains overflowing", and much of the sewer network is combined sewage and storm drains (Climate Change Strategy 2021). Coastal: the 2008 coastal storm surge flooded houses, and sea-defence repairs cost over £10 million (CCVRA 2022); revetments must withstand 1-in-200-year storms and new development must consider "a 0.5 m sea level rise" (Strategy 2021). Blocked private drains have flooded individual properties, and private drainage is "the legal responsibility of the property owner and NOT the Government" ([HMGoG PR 910/2022](https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/press-releases/tsd-advises-regular-maintenance-of-pipage-systems-9102022-8508)). No parcel-level flood map is published: check the plot against Strategy Figure 14 and ask the Technical Services Department about drainage at the address (2011–2022 data, source gibraltar.gov.gi; 2026-10-29 verified — figures may have changed since).

**Seismic**: two major historical earthquakes originated offshore south-west Iberia, west of the Strait (NCEI epicentre 37°N 10°W; USGS 36.0°N 10.6°W): the **1 Nov 1755 Lisbon earthquake** (Mw 8.5 in the NOAA NCEI database; its tsunami ran up 2.1 m at Gibraltar per NCEI's run-up record, and NCEI's event notes say "7 ft waves reported from Gibralter") and the **28 Feb 1969** earthquake off Cape St Vincent (Mw 7.8, "188 km SW of Sagres, Portugal" — USGS ISC-GEM catalogue). Territory-level seismic risk: est. MEDIUM-LOW to MEDIUM (skill assessment from the historical record, not an official zoning); the World Bank GFDRR [ThinkHazard](https://www.thinkhazard.org/en/report/95-gibraltar-u-k/EQ) tool (SHARE European hazard model) classifies Gibraltar's earthquake hazard as low: "a 2% chance of potentially-damaging earthquake shaking in your project area in the next 50 years". No Gibraltar-published seismic hazard map or seismic design rule was found in this check, so whether a building was designed for earthquake loads is unknown — ask the structural surveyor, especially for older Old Town / Casemates stock (2026-10-29 verified).

**Subsidence / geology**: the Rock is "interbedded dolomite and limestone bedrock from the Jurassic era that contain extensive cave systems", while the isthmus (airport and north) sits on marine sands and gravels, the Isthmus Sands (HMGoG [Gibraltar River Basin Management Plan 2015–2021](https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/new/sites/default/files/HMGoG_Documents/Gibraltar_River_Basin_Management_Plan_Public_Consultation_Main_Report.pdf)). The HMGoG [GeoPortal](https://www.geoportal.gov.gi/) lists 140+ caves and about 52 km of tunnels. **Rockfall and landslide**: the CCVRA 2022 rates mass movement medium probability and medium magnitude, expected to increase, notes that "much of the built environment is located at the base of the steep slopes of the Rock" and that rockfall nets are installed; the Strategy 2021 expects increased rock falls to "especially affect the eastern side of the Rock". Karst sinkholes and differential settlement are not quantified in any HMGoG source found — commission a structural survey, especially for older Town / Upper Rock stock and plots below cliffs (2015–2022 data, source gibraltar.gov.gi + geoportal.gov.gi; 2026-10-29 verified — figures may have changed since).

**Climate**: summers are dry and warm ("The summer drought frequently lasts 90 consecutive days"; August mean 24.3°C) and winters mixed ("On average only 30% of days are classified as wet"; January mean 13.5°C) (Gibraltar Met Office on the [HMGoG weather page](https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/gibraltar-tourist-board/weather)); the highest recorded temperature is "just topping 40°C" (CCVRA 2022). Heatwaves, drought, severe wind and coastal flooding are all expected to increase (CCVRA 2022 Table 2). Sea level: IPCC AR6 gives a likely global mean rise by 2100 (vs 1995–2014) of 0.28–0.55 m under SSP1-1.9 and 0.63–1.01 m under SSP5-8.5 ([WG1 SPM B.5.3](https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/wg1/downloads/report/IPCC_AR6_WGI_SPM.pdf), 2021); upgraded flood defences have been designed to withstand 0.5 m of sea-level rise, "in line with projections for the Mediterranean" (CCVRA 2022, citing HMGoG 2011). The Strategy 2021 says much of Gibraltar is low-lying land reclaimed from the sea and lists the hospital, airport runway, North Mole Power Station and "major residential areas & the beaches" as at risk from sea-level rise and storm surges — check reclaimed and low-lying plots against its Figure 14 (2021–2022 data; 2026-10-29 verified — figures may have changed since).

**Air quality**: HMGoG's [Air Quality Action Plan](https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/new/sites/default/files/HMGoG_Documents/Air%20Quality%20Action%20Plan.pdf) (Version I; PDF dated Oct 2010) names "power generation, road transport and shipping" as the main NOx sources and attributes 18.2% of measured PM10 to "industries around the Bay of Gibraltar in Spain" and 4.3% to shipping (Strait, Algeciras and Gibraltar port calls) — 2010 data, figures may have changed since, not least because the diesel power stations of that era gave way to the gas-fired North Mole plant (opened Sep 2019; Waterport decommissioned Dec 2020 — Strategy 2021). Monitoring started in April 2005 ([HMGoG Air Quality](https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/environment/air-quality)); near-real-time data at [gibraltarairquality.gi](https://www.gibraltarairquality.gi/) (offline for security maintenance on 2026-10-29).

**Crime** (Royal Gibraltar Police data in the [Gibraltar Police Authority Annual Report 2024-2025](https://www.gpa.gi/download/69), policing year 1 Apr–31 Mar): 3,395 recorded crimes in 2024/25 vs 3,440 in 2023/24 (−1.3%); 686 detected (= 20.2% of 3,395, computed; the report calls its detection rate "a snapshot in time" likely to rise 2–3 points as live investigations close); residential burglary 28 (48 in 2023/24), non-residential burglary 72 (72). Territory-wide figures only — no published street-level breakdown was found (2024/25 data; 2026-10-29 verified).

**Confidence**: MEDIUM — flood, coastal, rockfall, climate and air-quality statements rest on HMGoG documents (CCVRA 2022, Climate Change Strategy 2021, Air Quality Action Plan 2010, River Basin Management Plan 2015), several 4+ years old; seismic history on NOAA NCEI / USGS catalogues, but the seismic band is a skill estimate; crime figures are RGP data for 2024/25 (GPA); parcel-level exposure (flood, rockfall, subsidence) requires survey.

## Section: `--mains`

**Data sources**:
- **AquaGib Ltd** — [aquagib.gi](https://www.aquagib.gi/): potable water (metered), salt water (unmetered), sewage pumping, and electricity meter reading and billing for GEA, under licence; now 100% beneficially owned by HM Government of Gibraltar (two thirds through its company GAH Ltd) after HMGoG agreed on 19 Dec 2024 to buy all of NWG Commercial Solutions' shares (AquaGib corporate page; 2026-10-29 verified)
- **Gibraltar Electricity Authority (GEA)** — [gea.gi](https://www.gea.gi/): "currently, the only Electricity Utility Provider in Gibraltar", responsible for generation, distribution and supply; set up by the Gibraltar Electricity Authority Act 2003 (GEA site; 2026-10-29 verified)
- **No gas utility**: the Gibraltar Gas Company (founded 1857) supplied piped gas until the late 1960s ([Ministry for Heritage](https://www.ministryforheritage.gi/heritage-and-antiquities/gas-valve-no-3-612)); no current gas network operator was found (2026-10-29 check)
- **Telecoms / broadband**: Gibtelecom ([gibtele.com](https://www.gibtele.com/)) — the designated universal service provider and the exclusive mobile operator; fixed broadband also from GibFibre Ltd and u-mee (Broadband (Gibraltar) Ltd) ([GRA Annual Report 2025/26](https://www.gra.gi/uploads/documents/Home/Annual%20Reports/GRA%20Annual%20Report%202026.pdf)); regulator: [Gibraltar Regulatory Authority](https://www.gra.gi/communications)

**Notes**:
- **Water**: potable water is desalinated seawater (reverse osmosis): "The sea around Gibraltar is the source of both our potable and salt water supplies" (AquaGib). Gibraltar has no rivers, but it does have aquifers: isthmus wells were blended into supply until abstraction "ceased being operationally viable following the construction of the new airport terminal in 2011" (AquaGib history). Dual supply: a separate unmetered salt-water network for fire-fighting, street and sewer flushing and other sanitary uses; "Properties have a salt-water connection installed as part of certification" (AquaGib FAQ). Domestic potable tariff ([AquaGib](https://www.aquagib.gi/customer-service/tariffs-charges/); the page is headed "Effective date: 1 April 2026" but footnoted "Tariff effective date: 1 August 2022"): £0.289 per 100 litres for the first 4,500 litres a month, £0.719 per 100 litres above that, standing charge £3.48 a month, plus a £1 weekly levy per meter; connection administration fee £37.50 where a meter exists (2026-10-29 verified)
- **Sewer**: the [Technical Services Department](https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/transport-traffic-and-technical-services/technical-services-department) maintains "all public sewerage drainage systems (stormwater and sewers)"; AquaGib runs sewage pumping under licence; private drains are the owner's responsibility (HMGoG PR 910/2022). Much of the network is combined sewage and storm drains, and wastewater is discharged to the sea untreated (CCVRA 2022); a new sewage treatment plant (Europa Point, Ecowaters Ltd, tender awarded June 2025, pre-construction works from Oct 2025) "will become a reality in a couple of years" (Minister, 3 Oct 2025, [HMGoG PR 716/2025](https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/press-releases/pre-construction-works-commence-for-gibraltars-new-sewage-treatment-plant-at-europa-point-7162025-11282)). Confirm the plot's drainage with TSD and the surveyor (2026-10-29 verified)
- **Electricity**: generated on the territory — Gibraltar "works on 'island' mode with its own power generation and water desalination plants" (Climate Change Strategy 2021); the Strategy and GEA's history page mention no link to the Spanish grid, and the CCVRA 2022 says there is "no alternate supply to call on" if demand spikes cause shortages. The gas-fired North Mole power station (officially opened Sep 2019 per the Strategy; GEA says it "entered service in 2020" with about 80 MW installed) replaced Waterport (decommissioned Dec 2020). Domestic tariff 3 ([GEA tariff sheet](https://www.gea.gi/uploads/Downloads/200261026%20%20Electricity%20Tariffs%20and%20Charges%20amended.pdf), amended 26 Oct 2026, "apply as from April 2026"): £0.119 per unit plus a flexible cost adjustment of £0.058 per unit (= £0.177 per unit, computed), standing charge £5.80, weekly meter levy £1.00; application fee and domestic deposit £37.50 each (from Aug 2026). Mains: 240 V / 50 Hz with BS 1363 (Type G) sockets per secondary plug guides — not stated on the GEA pages checked; verify (2026-10-29 verified)
- **Gas**: no piped gas to homes — town gas ended in the late 1960s, "when the introduction of the electric cooker and the expansion of new housing estates made gas use redundant" ([Ministry for Heritage](https://www.ministryforheritage.gi/heritage-and-antiquities/gas-valve-no-3-612)); imported LNG fuels the North Mole power station, and Gibraltar's first LNG ship bunkering took place in March 2021 (Strategy 2021). Bottled LPG for cooking: no primary source found — ask the seller what the kitchen and water heater run on (2026-10-29 check)
- **Internet/fibre**: up to three residential fixed broadband providers, "all of whom provide at least some services via FTTH networks, with speeds ranging between 20Mbps and 2Gbps"; residential market share March 2026: GibFibre 44%, Gibtelecom 28%, u-mee 28%; 22,422 broadband subscribers in total (23,019 in March 2025); Gibtelecom is the only mobile operator ([GRA Annual Report 2025/26](https://www.gra.gi/uploads/documents/Home/Annual%20Reports/GRA%20Annual%20Report%202026.pdf); 2026-10-29 verified). Address-level availability is not published by GRA — ask each provider for the specific building

**Confidence**: HIGH for utility structure (AquaGib for water, GEA for electricity, three residential fixed broadband providers and one mobile operator — operator and GRA sources) and for the posted water and electricity tariffs; MEDIUM for parcel-level connection confirmation (always verify with seller's most recent bills); mains voltage / socket type rests on secondary plug guides only.

## Section: `--notary` / Conveyancing

**Data sources**:
- [Supreme Court of Gibraltar](https://www.gcs.gov.gi/) — civil registry
- [Gibraltar Courts Service — List of Practitioners, Notaries, etc.](https://www.gcs.gov.gi/list-of-practitioners-notaries-etc) — "List of Barristers and Solicitors 2026" + "Public Notaries 2026"; the list covers barristers, solicitors and public notaries; no separate licensed-conveyancer register is published by the Courts Service or the Law Council. Representative body: [Law Council of Gibraltar](https://lawcouncil.gi/) (includes the Gibraltar Bar Council); regulator: [LSRA](https://lsra.gi/) (2026 data, source gcs.gov.gi; 2026-09-18 verified)

**Process**:

1. **Offer accepted** → instruct a **Gibraltar-qualified barrister or solicitor**. There is no "notary" institution in the civil-law sense — Gibraltar uses common-law conveyancing.
2. **Pre-contract enquiries**: title, planning, search of the **Land Titles Registry (LPS)**, Crown lease consents (if applicable), **market-class designation** (Open vs Restricted)
3. **Exchange of contracts** with deposit (typically 10%)
4. **Stamp Duty assessment** by HM Commissioner of Stamp Duty under the Stamp Duties Act
5. **Completion** + registration of the new title at the **Land Titles Registry (LPS)**
6. **Conveyancing fees**: typically **1–1.5% of price + disbursements** for residential — verify with quoted firm

**Foreign-buyer extras**:
- Gibraltar-based AML/KYC checks under GFSC and Financial Services (Implementation of EU Anti-Money Laundering Directive) Regulations — source of funds documentation mandatory
- Non-EU buyers: residency permit application is **separate from purchase** and runs in parallel (often pre-completion to ensure occupancy)
- Post-Brexit, EU/EEA buyers face the same documentary regime as third-country nationals
- **Restricted-Market eligibility evidence** (3-year residency) required at exchange if the property sits in that tier

**Typical timeline**: 6–12 weeks from offer to completion (longer for complex Crown lease properties).

**Confidence**: HIGH on legal framework (common-law conveyancing, **Land Titles Registry (LPS)** registration); MEDIUM on fee ranges (firm-to-firm variation).

## Section: `--frontier-treaty` (UK–EU Gibraltar Treaty)

**Status as of 2026-07-15** (facts researched + dual-verified 2026-07-02):

- Formal instrument: *Agreement in respect of Gibraltar between the European Union and the European Atomic Energy Community, of the one part, and the United Kingdom of Great Britain and Northern Ireland, of the other part* ([COM(2026) 91 final](https://www.exteriores.gob.es/es/PoliticaExterior/Documents/COM_2026_91_1_EN_annexe_proposition_cp_part1_v5.pdf))
- Draft text published **26 February 2026** ([UK Government + EU Commission joint publication](https://commonslibrary.parliament.uk/research-briefings/cbp-10572/))
- **EU Council greenlight: 1 April 2026** ([Consilium press release](https://www.consilium.europa.eu/en/press/press-releases/2026/04/01/eu-uk-relations-member-states-greenlight-eu-uk-deal-on-gibraltar/)); Council formally adopted the signature + provisional-application decisions by written procedure ~1 Jul 2026 (per [Gibraltar Chronicle](https://www.chronicle.gi/eu-completes-formal-adoption-of-treaty-and-clears-way-for-signing) — secondary; no primary Council press release located for the written-procedure step)
- **In PROVISIONAL APPLICATION since 15 July 2026** (Article 336 of the Agreement; date + frontier arrangements confirmed by [HMGoG press release 517/2026 of 2 Jul 2026](https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/press-releases/new-frontier-security-measures-in-place-as-government-publishes-plans-for-the-border-area-5172026-12156))
- **NOT yet ratified**: European Parliament consent pending — procedure 2026/0058(NLE), joint AFET/INTA, awaiting committee decision as of 2026-10-21 per [OEIL](https://oeil.europarl.europa.eu/oeil/en/procedure-file?reference=2026/0058(NLE)) — indicative plenary 14 Dec 2026 ([EPRS briefing 789376](https://www.europarl.europa.eu/RegData/etudes/BRIE/2026/789376/EPRS_BRI(2026)789376_EN.pdf)); UK Constitutional Reform and Governance Act 2010 scrutiny — treaty laid 14 Jul 2026, first 21-sitting-day objection period ends 22 Oct 2026 ([Commons Library CBP-10980](https://commonslibrary.parliament.uk/research-briefings/cbp-10980/)) ([Commons Library CBP-10572](https://commonslibrary.parliament.uk/research-briefings/cbp-10572/))
- Source: [European Commission proposal IP/26/428](https://ec.europa.eu/commission/presscorner/detail/en/ip_26_428) · [FCDO treaty summary](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/draft-uk-eu-agreement-in-respect-of-gibraltar/summary-draft-uk-eu-agreement-in-respect-of-gibraltar-accessible)

**Provisions affecting property buyers** (in effect since 15 Jul 2026):
- Schengen border checks **relocated from land frontier (La Verja / La Línea) to Gibraltar airport + port** — dual controls: Gibraltar manages immigration + policing, Spanish officials are responsible for Schengen-area integrity (FCDO summary — likened to French officers at London St Pancras)
- Land frontier: routine vehicle / pedestrian immigration checks and physical barriers removed; old fence replaced by category-4 "no-climb" fencing sea-to-airport with CCTV, live facial recognition + ANPR; *"Initially there will be little change to traffic flow"* (HMGoG PR 517/2026, 2 Jul 2026)
- Gibraltar remains **outside both Schengen and the EU**; Article 2 of the Agreement: nothing affects sovereignty (FCDO summary §8)
- Customs union between EU and Gibraltar for goods movement; 3-year travellers'-allowance transition — a Gibraltar resident entering by land with ≤ €300 of purchased goods pays EU VAT rather than Gibraltar transaction tax; goods whose movement to Gibraltar started before 15 Jul 2026 fall **outside** the treaty even if they arrive after ([HMGoG treaty portal](https://treaty-gov.info) — official HMGoG microsite, © HMGoG; canonical status worth confirming at gibraltar.gov.gi)
- **Gibraltar residents exempt from EU Entry/Exit System (EES) wet-stamping + ETIAS**, whatever Civilian Registration Card colour (Government of Gibraltar press releases 148/2026 + 234/2026; Agreement Art. 42: no entry/exit stamps, EES and ETIAS exemption for persons resident in Gibraltar). ⚠️ In practice some residents were wrongly registered in EES after 15 Jul 2026; the European Commission has since instructed Schengen authorities not to register them, and an affected resident can ask the Schengen State of their last recorded crossing to erase the record (HMGoG Technical Notices 696/2026 of 23 Sep 2026 and 778/2026 of 13 Oct 2026). Carry your passport plus Gibraltar ID or civilian registration card (2026-10-21 verified)
- ⚠️ **Gap**: whether NON-resident British passengers at the airport undergo full EES enrolment (fingerprints + facial scan) is asserted by secondary sources but NOT confirmed in published government guidance — verify current airport guidance before travel
- **Treaty does NOT grant EU free movement to Gibraltar** — verbatim Government of Gibraltar release 148/2026: *"Those wishing to reside in Gibraltar will continue to be subject to Gibraltar's own immigration laws and procedures."* Residency permit + Cat-2 / HEPSS / employment / self-sufficiency route still required for occupancy. Responsibility for determining residency remains with the Government of Gibraltar. The treaty does add **coordinated procedures for the approval of new or renewed residence permits that grant Schengen access** (FCDO summary §14). Under the signed text, Gibraltar still issues the permits, but **Art. 50** requires it to notify Spain before issuing or renewing one. Spain then has 28 days (extendable by 14) to object on public policy, internal security, public health or international relations grounds, including a SIS alert. If it objects, the permit is not issued; silence counts as no objection. A residence permit also needs a **genuine connection** (actual and regular physical presence or other objective criteria — Art. 45(4)-(5)), and **Art. 45(6) bars meeting that condition through predetermined investments in Gibraltar's economy or real estate, or predetermined payments to the Gibraltar authorities** — buying property cannot itself be the basis for residence (UK-EU Agreement, MS No.9/2026; 2026-10-21 verified).

**Absent-Cat-2 enforcement caveat**: the treaty does **not** override Gibraltar's domestic Cat-2 / HEPSS residency conditions. Cat-2 holders are required to **maintain approved Gibraltar accommodation for the whole year of assessment** and to physically reside there — relocating the Schengen border to airport/port does **not** relax that physical-presence test. Community signal (r/gibraltar treaty thread Q2 2026, "All that property owned by absent tax residents who now can't be residents…") suggests treaty implementation may tighten **enforcement** against absent-tax-resident Cat-2 holders. Re-confirm Cat-2 occupancy compliance post-15-Jul-2026 with Gibraltar Finance and (if non-trivial) a Gibraltar-licensed tax advisor.

**Domestic residence rules are tightening IN PARALLEL (separate from the treaty)**: HMGoG announced a new **Residence Criteria framework** on 17 Jun 2026 ([press release 466/2026](https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/press-releases/government-announces-new-residence-criteria-framework-4662026-12103)) — residency to be based on *"genuine economic contribution, regulatory compliance"* and a demonstrable genuine connection; **residence alone does not grant the wider social benefits reserved for Gibraltarian Status**; now in force as the **Residency Regulations 2026** (Legal Notice 166 of 2026, Gazette 9 Jul 2026, in operation 14 Jul 2026 — [parliament.gi](https://www.parliament.gi/uploads/contents/papers_laid/2026/Residency%20Regulations%202026.pdf)). The [HMGoG treaty portal](https://treaty-gov.info) said on 2026-07-02 that the **3-month-then-register** rule for British/EU citizens was unchanged by the treaty, but the Residency Regulations 2026 repeal IARA ss.18-34 and Part IV (reg. 26; Withdrawal Agreement rights saved, reg. 27) and penalise residing without a valid permit at £2,500 (reg. 22) — whether any 3-month grace survives is not stated in the Regulations; verify with the Department of Immigration and Home Affairs (2026-10-21 verified). Cat-2 changes are primary-confirmed by [HMGoG PR 469/2026](https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/press-releases/government-announces-changes-to-category-2-regime-4692026-12107) (18 Jun 2026: new-applicant net wealth £2M → £5M, fee £1,233 → £5,000, existing holders grandfathered); PR 469/2026 does not mention HEPSS; only the fee change is legislated (LN 127/2026). Under the Residency Regulations, employee and self-employed applicants (55 or under, earning at least Gibraltar average gross annual earnings — both waivable by the Chief Minister for important skills or a shortage sector) must show a purchase or rental agreement for a Gibraltar property used as their primary residence (not a vessel or holiday rental; rentals at least 12 months; may be conditional on the permit). Permits last one year (fee £250, renewal £20), and residing without one carries a £2,500 penalty. HMGoG PR 813/2026 (21 Oct 2026) announced, not yet made, an amendment adding a route for entirely self-sufficient over-55s (private medical insurance, a health-system contribution, at least six months a year in Gibraltar). (2026-10-21 verified)

**Verification path** (essential — re-check before purchase):
- [gibraltar.gov.gi/press-releases](https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/press-releases) for Government-of-Gibraltar implementation announcements
- [HMGoG treaty portal](https://treaty-gov.info) — official implementation guidance (transition rules, frontier arrangements)
- [House of Commons Library briefing CBP-10572](https://commonslibrary.parliament.uk/research-briefings/cbp-10572/) — UK Parliament background

**Confidence**: MEDIUM-HIGH — provisional application is in force (multiple primary sources incl. HMGoG PR 517/2026 of 2 Jul 2026 + FCDO summary); operational specifics (traffic layout, EES handling for non-resident British visitors) still settling; full ratification pending (European Parliament consent — indicative plenary 14 Dec 2026 + UK CRAG, first objection period ends 22 Oct 2026). Facts researched + dual-verified 2026-07-02. Re-verify at gov.gi closer to purchase date.

## Section: `--integrity`

**Anti-hallucination notes specific to Gibraltar**:

1. **Don't conflate with UK**: Gibraltar tax (no VAT, no IHT, no CGT) ≠ UK tax (20% VAT, 40% IHT, CGT). Gibraltar has its own Income Tax Office, NOT HMRC.
2. **Don't conflate with Spain**: Spain's IBI / Plusvalía / Impuesto sobre el Patrimonio do **not** apply in Gibraltar.
3. **Don't conflate Gibraltar pound (GIP) with GBP**: 1:1 peg, but GIP banknotes are **not legal tender in UK** — convert before travel.
4. **Brexit timing**: Gibraltar left EU **31 January 2020** with UK. EU/EEA pre-Brexit access rules no longer apply. Use 2025–2026 primary sources, not 2020-era news.
5. **Frontier Treaty timing**: **provisionally applied since 15 July 2026** — pre-treaty arrangements (e.g. EES border checks from April 2026) superseded at the land frontier; **ratification still pending** (EP consent — indicative plenary 14 Dec 2026; UK CRAG first objection period ends 22 Oct 2026), so do not describe the treaty as "ratified"; re-verify operational status.
6. **TWO-TIER market is real and load-bearing**: "no nationality restriction" applies to Open Market only. Restricted Market = 3-year prior Gibraltar residency, owner-occupation only, est. ~half of stock (per estate-agent signposting — no published register split). Never describe Gibraltar as "no foreign-ownership restrictions" without the Open / Restricted caveat.
7. **Cat-2 net worth**: **£5M for new applicants** per HMGoG PR 469/2026 (18 Jun 2026; existing holders grandfathered); the former £2M was confirmed verbatim by Hassans, but neither figure is in the Rules — the consolidated Rules (22 Jun 2026) require only 'substantial and sound financial standing' (rule 6(a)), and LN 127/2026 changed only the fee — *"potential applicants must have at least an estimated total net worth in excess of £2 million"*. The Gibraltar Income Tax Office page itself states the income cap (£118k) and tax band (£37k–£42,380) but does not restate the £2M figure publicly — re-confirm at the Gibraltar Finance Centre at application.
8. **HEPSS vs Cat-2 are distinct schemes**: HEPSS = employed senior executives with skills not locally available (£160k earnings floor, £160k assessable cap, £39,940 fixed tax, 3-year non-residency window). Cat-2 = high-net-worth passive residents (£5M net worth for new applicants per PR 469/2026, £118k income cap, £37k–£42,380 tax band, 5-year non-residency window). Don't conflate.
9. **Mortgage stamp duty applies even to small loans**: 0.13% on secured sums up to £200k vs 0.20% above £200k. Easy to elide vs the property stamp duty bands.
10. **Treaty does NOT relax Cat-2 physical-presence test**: relocating Schengen checks to airport/port has zero effect on Cat-2 / HEPSS accommodation-and-presence conditions. Absent-tax-resident enforcement may in fact tighten post-15-Jul-2026 — and HMGoG's domestic Residence Criteria framework (PR 466/2026, 17 Jun 2026) is in operation as the Residency Regulations 2026 (LN 166/2026, from 14 Jul 2026).

## Caveats unique to Gibraltar

- **Two-tier residential property market** (Open / Restricted) — non-residents categorically limited to Open Market; est. ~half of stock by unit count is Restricted (per estate-agent signposting — no published register split; 3-year prior residency, owner-occupation only)
- **Single-postcode addressing** (GX11 1AA territory-wide) — locator data must be parcel/street-level, postcode is not a discriminator
- **Crown-leasehold ubiquity**: most central Gibraltar developed property is on long Crown lease (99–150yr); freehold is uncommon
- **Affordable Housing buyback**: 7.5% vendor-paid stamp duty on resale within 10 years at Beach View Terraces / Mons Calpe Mews only (Stamp Duties Act s.19B) + 10-year Government buyback right on subsidised schemes (sits in Restricted Market tier)
- **Tax-residency-of-origin overhang**: UK-domiciled buyers retain UK IHT exposure on Gibraltar property unless domicile changes (not a Gibraltar issue, but commonly misunderstood)
- **Frontier Treaty (provisionally applied since 15 Jul 2026)** changes commuting & border friction but does not itself grant residence — Gibraltar's own residency law applies, now the Residency Regulations 2026 (LN 166/2026, in operation 14 Jul 2026, made under the Treaty on Gibraltar and the European Union Act 2026) — and does NOT relax Cat-2 physical-presence
- **Cat-2 / HEPSS thresholds change with annual budget** — always re-verify with Gibraltar Finance Centre directly; Residency Regulations 2026 (LN 166/2026) in operation since 14 Jul 2026 (framework announced PR 466/2026); Cat-2 £5,000 fee legislated (LN 127/2026, 22 Jun 2026); new-applicant net wealth £5M announced per PR 469/2026 (18 Jun 2026), administrative, not in the Rules text
- **Self-employment work-permit route is discretionary and frequently refused** — do not rely on a property lease/purchase as evidence of eligibility

## Quirks to know

- **Llanito** local code-switching English/Spanish is everyday; legal/transactional documentation is **English only**
- **British driving licence + UK passport are sufficient** for UK nationals; EU nationals need full residency stack post-Brexit
- **No piped gas** — the last town-gas supply ended in the late 1960s ([Ministry for Heritage](https://www.ministryforheritage.gi/heritage-and-antiquities/gas-valve-no-3-612)); check whether the kitchen and water heater run on electricity or bottled gas; factor in if relocating from a UK gas-heated home
- **Property surveys**: RICS-registered surveyors do operate in Gibraltar; engaging a UK surveyor on a Gibraltar property may need local-knowledge supplementation (Crown lease, karst geology)
- **No public price-paid database or official house-price index** — the Land Titles Register keeps "a public record of property transactions", but online title searches go through the LPS Land Registry Web Portal, "offered, for a fee, to property professionals" (account by application; LPS lists portal access as restricted to Gibraltar-based professionals); the Statistics Office's 2026 topic tables have no house-price series, and its "Property Sales" tables count only sales crediting the Improvement and Development Fund or Government-owned property companies (Treasury), not market-wide transactions. Comparables come from estate agents and listing portals (asking prices — per listing, verify) (less open than UK / FR) (2026-10-29 verified; sources [LPS Land Registry](https://www.lps.gi/land-registry), [LPS Web Portal](https://www.lps.gi/land-registry-dept), [LPS Downloads](https://www.lps.gi/downloads), [Statistics — Property Sales 2026](https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/statistics/statistics-topic-area/2026/property-sales))
